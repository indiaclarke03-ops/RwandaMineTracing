# Two-country mineral balance: DRC and Rwanda (run 2026-11-04)

Data: `balance.json`. Everything in the bridge is tier **inferred**: our analysis, not a sourced finding. Rwanda denies supporting M23 and denies laundering. It did not answer the UN request of 2 Apr 2026 (S/2026/466 para 225). Customs and balance data can show consistency, not where ore was mined.

## Short answer

- **Coltan: yes, broadly.** Official North and South Kivu output fell by 486, 910 and 1,350 t in 2023-25 against its 2020-22 mean of 1,393 t. Rwanda's excess was 763, 1,044 and 1,444 t. The Kivu loss covers 64%, 87% and 93% of the excess.
- **A national "missing output" does not exist.** Recorded DRC coltan exports exceed recorded production in 2022-24. The gap only shows up province by province.
- **Cassiterite: less than half of the headline rise looks like concentrate.** 1,620 t of the 2025 rise went to Cambodia at $1.6/kg, against about $20/kg elsewhere. The concentrate rise is about 1,221 t, not 2,841 t.
- **Growth went to China.** China took all of the net coltan growth on the mirror, and its share rose from 53% in 2022 to 79% in 2024. Thailand fell from 550 t to 10 t.
- **Official records do not show the flow.** The DRC reports no 3T exports to Rwanda in any year. Rwanda reports cassiterite imports from the DRC of 147-299 t in 2021-24, and none in 2025.

Methods:
- **Official output and exports** are SG Mines. CTCPM and USGS disagree: 2,849 t coltan in 2024. DRC Comtrade (1,098 t) sits close to SG Mines (1,245 t).
- **Kivu loss, coltan:** 2020-22 mean official North + South Kivu output minus the year's output.
- **Kivu loss, tin:** artisanal exports 3,349 t (2024) minus 76 t (2025). **Tungsten:** Kivu exports lost year on year.
- **Coltan 2025 excess:** exports 2,743 t minus 1/3 of cassiterite frozen at the 2022-24 Comtrade mean of 3,897 t = 1,444 t.
- **Variants:** BNR cassiterite baseline 4,634 t gives 1,198 t. Same-year ratio with Cambodia removed gives 1,167 t. The old unfrozen figure is 627 t.
- **Tungsten 2025:** Comtrade says +280 t over the 2022-24 mean, but BNR to November (2,283 t) suggests no rise. Treat it as unresolved.
- **Gold:** no shortfall or excess computed. Rwanda's exports cannot be split into declared re-exports and DRC gold.

On official figures the total is flat from 2023 and Rwanda's share rises, which fits reallocation. China's mirror tells a different story: imports from both countries more than double, and imports from the DRC fall only modestly. So some of the official DRC fall may be a recording problem, and total regional supply may have grown (the UN says Rubaya output rose, para 221).

Pass-through rates of 50, 75 and 100% are our judgement. They stand in for ore taking other routes, entering the baseline, or lagging. No source gives them. "Armed-group-held" is not the same as M23: Rubaya was held by PARECO-FF/Wazalendo until 30 Apr 2024, and about 500 t in 2025 was looted exporter stock.

Cross-checks: Global Witness says at least 1,400 t in the first year. ITSCI recorded 2,038 t of tantalum against 3,264 t of world imports from Rwanda in 2025, a gap of 1,226 t, close to the 1,167-1,444 t range here.

**What would falsify this:**
- Mine-level Rwandan production records that show the rise. Rwanda publishes none.
- Grade or analytical-fingerprint tests tying Rwandan exports to Rwandan pits.
- A documented stock drawdown, or a non-Kivu source of matching size (Burundi, Uganda, Maniema).
- Rubaya output shown leaving by another route.
- Official North Kivu output recovering while Rwandan exports stay high.

## 6. Sayari

- **Coverage:** no Rwandan or DRC customs source. Tanzania-heavy, and date filters are ignored. No Tanzania COD-departure rows after Nov 2024 although official DRC exports continued, so that is a coverage break.
- **DRC to Rwanda:** one coltan row (26 t, Nov 2025, Congo Ressources Mining to Meta Trading Minerals) and no tin rows.
- **Same buyers on both sides:** by entity ID, 24% of COD coltan weight and 22% of COD tin weight went to buyers that also buy from Rwanda: Halcyon (369 t COD coltan), Traxys, Tata International Singapore, Minterra, East Rise. A shared buyer shows a shared network, not shared ore. Halcyon denies its coltan came from Rubaya.
- **Buyer concentration, Rwanda coltan:** HHI 0.08 (2023) to 0.15 (2025). Minterra alone took 933 t of covered 2025 weight, up from 99 t in 2023.
- **East Rise (new):** trade node [East Rise Corporation Ltd](https://graph.sayari.com/resource/entity/vdCIJ54CEY7kXe8uUJMsvg) shares the Wu Sang House address of OFAC-listed [East Rise Corporation Limited](https://graph.sayari.com/resource/entity/RXGsi4gAb93ZNWFwc4MkPA) (SDN 55048). That resolves the "unconfirmed" note in `gap_sayari_trade`, with moderate-to-high confidence. It received DRC coltan from CDMC-named shippers (2023-24) and 66 rows, 1,408 t, of 3T from Rwanda. The last Rwanda row is dated 12 Aug 2025, the day of the Treasury action. [CDMC](https://graph.sayari.com/resource/entity/4WRQGD0jznrVt2MR5c4OBg) is SDN 55047, but the shipper nodes are unflagged and matched by name. Rows before designation are not a breach.
- **Cambodia (new):** a Rwandan smelter to [a Cambodian buyer](https://graph.sayari.com/resource/entity/XR7OTPJXfva4L5DqaEKkMQ), 45 rows, 1,632 t, Sep 2025 to Feb 2026. It also sits awkwardly with para 224.

## 7. Caveats

- Everything in `bridge` and every row with source analysis_balance is tier INFERRED: our analysis, not a sourced finding. No source states these shares. Rwanda denies supporting M23 and denies laundering; it did not answer the UN request of 2 Apr 2026 (S/2026/466 para 225). Customs and balance data show consistency, not origin.
- Cambodia tonnage: Rwanda reports 1,620 t of HS 260900 to Cambodia in 2025 at $1.6/kg, against about $20/kg to every other partner. Sayari shows 45 rows (1,632 t, Sep 2025 to Feb 2026) from a Rwandan smelter to a Cambodian buyer (nodes XR7OTPJXfva4L5DqaEKkMQ and kW9reTuI47NN8_2QvD_c9g), some described as tin-containing materials. BNR unit value falls from about $22/kg to $13/kg in Oct-Nov 2025, and the arithmetic fits: Oct 640 t low-value + 809 t at $23 gives about $19.6m (BNR $19.25m); Nov 408 t + 542 t gives about $13.1m (BNR $12.95m). So the October 2025 spike of about 1,450 t that the UN flags (annex 70) looks substantially low-value material, not concentrate. This is our inference: the material could be slag, residues or mis-valued ore, and we have not seen a document. It also sits awkwardly with S/2026/466 para 224, which says the only tin refinery reported no significant export rise. This should go to the UN Group and the smelter for comment before publication; the smelter reports RMI-conformant status, and nothing here alleges wrongdoing.
- Rwanda reported cassiterite imports from COD of 147, 248, 217 and 299 t in 2021-24, and 0 t in 2025, when exports surged. Coltan and tungsten: effectively none in any year. The DRC reports no 3T exports to Rwanda in any year; gold rows are a few kg. So official records on both sides do not capture this flow.
- Zero-sum test: Rwanda plus DRC official coltan exports were 3,214, 3,949, 3,629 and 3,634 t in 2022-25, while Rwanda share rose from 41% to 75%. From 2023 the total is about flat and DRC falls as Rwanda rises, consistent with reallocation. But China mirror imports from both rose from 1,690 to 3,852 t, so if the mirror is right, total regional supply grew and DRC official exports are understated. China mirror from COD fell only from 1,203 t (2023) to 987 t (2025), much less than official DRC exports. Part of the DRC recorded fall may be a books problem.
- DRC official statistics are weak and split into two series that disagree: SG Mines coltan exports 2024 = 1,244.86 t; CTCPM/USGS = 2,849 t, of which 1,353 t is a Haut-Katanga figure IPIS links to a September spike. This file uses SG Mines for the Kivu series because it gives monthly exit-post tables.
- USGS DRC gross tonnages are footnoted "Reported exports", the same circularity as Rwanda. There is no independent DRC production series.
- National production minus exports is negative for coltan in 2022-24: recorded exports exceed recorded output, so a simple national shortfall does not exist. The useful measure is the Kivu loss: official output leaving the state record. It is a measure of ore lost to the books, not proof that it reached Rwanda, and real output may have fallen.
- Baseline mixes sources: 2020-21 EITI, 2022 USGS citing the ministry, 2023-25 SG Mines. Coltan South Kivu 2024 is 349 t (SG) or 543 t (USGS/CTCPM).
- The ministry says North and South Kivu output was sent to Kindu and Kalemie for export. Some Kivu loss therefore left through Maniema or Tanganyika rather than Rwanda: at most about 182 t of coltan exports booked to Maniema in 2025. Maniema coltan output jumps from 0 t to 446 t (282 t in Dec 2025) and Haut-Lomami has a 382 t June spike; neither is explained.
- SG Mines 2025 Table 52 prints 1,180.99 t as coltan exports. Its province values are the production values (Table 39), so it appears mislabelled; we use Table 51 (891.31 t), the monthly exit-post table. The restated 2024 figure of 1,367.12 t adds 122.26 t from an erroneous Q3 cell; we use 1,244.86 t.
- The 25% geology ratio is one number (BGR via UN annex 53) for mixed deposits. The excess moves a lot with it, and using cassiterite as the baseline assumes Rwandan cassiterite itself is domestic. If the pre-2023 baseline already contained smuggled ore, the excess is understated.
- The frozen baseline assumes the 2025 cassiterite surge is not domestic. If it is domestic, the 627 t figure stands.
- Armed-area volumes can exceed the excess without contradiction: part of the ore may sit inside the baseline, and exports lag mining. The 50/75/100% pass-through rates are our judgement, not measured. Other routes (Uganda, Burundi) are reported but not sized.
- Share of ore from armed-group-held areas is not the same as M23 ore. Before 30 Apr 2024 Rubaya was under PARECO-FF/Wazalendo. About 500 t of 2025 flow was looted exporter stock (S/2025/446), not fresh mining.
- Comtrade cassiterite is below BNR in every year since 2019; the cause is unresolved. UN 2025 figure 7,700 t is BNR; Comtrade says 6,349 t. Tungsten 2025: Comtrade 3,009 t vs BNR 2,283 t to November.
- Mirror data for Rwanda 2025 is partial. Malaysia coltan 2021 and 2025 at about $4-5/kg are shown in `partners` with excluded=true and left out of totals and growth. China, as importer, reports CIF and origin may reflect the shipper, so Rwanda-origin in China mirror data is a declaration.
- Trading hubs (UAE, Hong Kong, Luxembourg, Switzerland) are domiciles, not processing sites. Sayari arrival country can differ from buyer domicile: UAE buyers increasingly take title while ore lands in China.
- Sayari: no Rwandan or DRC customs source. Rows are Tanzania-heavy third-country records. Sayari ignored date filters. Offset paging returned 500 of 512 tin rows. No COD-departure tin or coltan row from Tanzania customs after Nov 2024, although official DRC exports continued, so this is a coverage break and not a trade stop. Do not read the 2025 fall in COD rows as a real fall. Buyer names are not resolved to legal entities; shared names are leads only.
- A few DRC rows name the same buyers as Rwandan rows (Halcyon, Traxys, East Rise, Minterra, Tata International). A shared buyer shows a shared trading network, not shared ore. Halcyon denied that its coltan came from Rubaya (Global Witness, Jun 2026); Rwandan smelter-compliance findings and the Ulba and Taniobis denials stand.
- Comtrade preview is capped at 500 rows; no call hit the cap. DRC Comtrade is sparse and swings: tin 18,468 t in 2023 vs 1,854 t in 2022, against about 33,000 t official.
- East Rise: Sayari node vdCIJ54CEY7kXe8uUJMsvg (unflagged) has the same Hong Kong address as OFAC-designated East Rise Corporation Limited (SDN 55048, HK CR 1585306), which we treat as the same legal entity with moderate-to-high confidence: name, address and trade route match; no registry number on the trade node. This updates the "unconfirmed" note in gap_sayari_trade. 66 Rwanda-departure rows (1,408 t across HS 261590, 260900, 261100) run from Jan 2023 to 2025-08-12, the day of the Treasury action, and none follow, though Tanzania coverage runs to Jul 2026. Rows before designation are not a sanctions breach. The designation is tier designated; the trade link is inferred. Name variants E.R.C LTD are matched by pattern and are lower confidence.

## 8. Not done or not found

- No monthly or quarterly ITSCI, IPIS or Reuters series for Kivu exports was found. The monthly figures here are ministry tables.
- China monthly mirror data on the preview API stops at Dec 2024, so it cannot test the post-Goma months.
- Province data for 2023 cassiterite was not found. DRC 2024 Comtrade tin and gold weights are rejected as reporting errors.
- USGS MYB 2023 was not available at the expected URL. The Ecofin article was not read, so its 2,485 t figure is not used.
