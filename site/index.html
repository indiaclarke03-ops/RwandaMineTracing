An investigation · Rwanda and eastern DR Congo ·

The metals in your phone, and the mines they come from

Tantalum, tin and gold make modern life work. A large share is dug by hand in eastern Congo, where armed groups control many of the mines and the people who work in them. This is how those metals reach you, what it costs the miners, and whether anything done so far has helped.

How to read the evidence

◆Designated
A government has acted on it, in US, EU or UN sanctions. Still that government's finding.
■Documented
Set out with evidence by UN investigators or an investigative NGO, or in official statistics. Not a court finding.
●Reported
Reported by the media.
△Inferred
Our own analysis. It always says how it was worked out, and it is not proof.

How to read this map
  • Ring on a labelled site: control claim (thicker = stronger evidence)
  • Dashed ring: media-reported only

Labelled markers are sites researched in depth. Dots are mining sites surveyed by IPIS. Fainter dots were last visited over four years before the selected date. A visit shows who was present that day, not who controls the site now.
