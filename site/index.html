Case: Rwanda and eastern DR Congo ·

An armed group controls major coltan mines in eastern Congo. This tool follows what comes out of them.

Tantalum, tin and gold from eastern Congo end up in phones, laptops, cars and jewellery. Since 2024 the M23 armed group has seized mining areas there and taxed what they produce. UN investigators say much of that output is smuggled into neighbouring Rwanda and exported as Rwandan. Ground Control maps who controls each site, traces the minerals through traders, exporters and smelters, and checks whether sanctions and industry action have changed anything.

Checking a supplier? Search a company to see its shortest sourced path to an armed group, or follow a mine's output through the supply chain. Working on policy? Start with the responses. See every sanction, peace deal and industry suspension since 2024, and whether the indicators it was meant to move actually moved. Following the numbers? Start with the mineral balance: what Rwanda exports against what it can mine, what Congo stopped recording, and where the difference shipped.

How to read the evidence

Control in eastern Congo is contested and changes fast, and many claims here are allegations. Every claim carries one of four labels so you can see who is making it.

◆Designated
A government has acted on it: named in US, EU or UN sanctions. The strongest label, but it is still that government's finding.
■Documented
Set out with evidence by UN investigators or an investigative NGO such as Global Witness or Amnesty. Not a court finding.
●Reported
Reported by the media. Useful, but often relies on one or two sources.
△Inferred
Our own analysis, such as comparing export figures with production. Always says how it was worked out. It is not proof.

Where a named party denies a claim, the denial is shown next to it. More on the method.
